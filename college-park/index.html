How College Park Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually includes the month and year it was built, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that point and also shows discolored water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Common Water Heater Problems in College Park

Whatever type of water heater your College Park home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tanks tend to have trouble with pilot assemblies and thermocouples, dirty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric units lose heating elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless units throw error codes for scale buildup, ignition faults, and flow sensor problems, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Hybrid heat pump units depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they struggle in cramped closets. Our technicians work on all four every week and carry the parts to fix most problems on the first visit.
