Keeping Your Gaithersburg Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year if their water is hard.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs like discolored hot water, popping and rumbling, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and far less stressful than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble needs only a flush, yet that same buildup, neglected for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone now so help is one call away as soon as something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
