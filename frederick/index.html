How Frederick Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually includes the manufacture date, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that point and also shows rusty water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement.
